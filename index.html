THE CLINICAL NEED

Surveillance that repeats for years

Non-muscle-invasive bladder cancer (NMIBC) can recur after treatment. Patients may therefore enter long-term surveillance programmes, with repeated follow-up tailored to their risk. Cystoscopy remains central to current care.

This motivates research into practical, non-invasive approaches that could complement existing monitoring pathways. NOVINEXT is investigating a urine-based molecular concept; its clinical value has not yet been established.

Non-invasive

Urine sample collection

Longitudinal

Repeat monitoring as an intended use

Clinical context: EAU follow-up guidelines

TECHNOLOGY

From sample to interpretable result

The concept follows a single, transparent chain. Each step is being designed and evaluated in ongoing development work.

01

Urine sample

A non-invasive sample, with the final collection workflow still to be defined.

02

RNA biomarkers

Candidate RNA signatures being investigated for the intended monitoring use.

03

Molecular detection

A targeted assay design under evaluation. Analytical performance remains to be established.

04

Interpretable result

A proposed output for future clinical review, subject to validation.

WHO IT IS FOR

Built around the NMIBC surveillance pathway

The intended setting is hospitals, urology clinics and diagnostic laboratories involved in NMIBC recurrence surveillance.

Hospitals, clinics & labs

WHERE WE ARE

Early development, stated plainly

The product concept is defined and biomarker discovery is ongoing. The diagnostic approach is being designed and evaluated. Clinical and complete analytical validation are not yet finished.
